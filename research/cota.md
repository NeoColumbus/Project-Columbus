## Computational Study / Source Check 2026-10-07

The independent route-optimization study now has a frozen research record (`cd03af9c`, `research-final`) and a public working paper. Reporting revision checked: `8c1983b6bb0db1b9fb4ec49f67be7d346e38cf90`. This note summarizes published artifacts; it is not an independent reproduction or external peer review.

**FACT (reported model output):** Frequency reallocation on existing routes at approximately fixed modeled resources served about 680 additional modeled weekday trips (+3.3% served), reduced modeled unserved demand 6.65%, and increased total generalized travel cost 0.88%. The fixed-plan gain remained positive across 44 pre-specified perturbations. These are modeled effects, not observed ridership gains.

**ANALYSIS:** Existing-route frequency allocation warrants closer investigation. Through-routing had no supportable gain, local route edits had small effects, and the best of 200 promoted/certified greenfield candidates remained worse than the lightly edited existing network under matched comparisons. This is not a universal ranking of possible networks.

**PROPOSAL:** Validate against actual ridership and rider needs, then check fleet, staffing, and operational feasibility before proposing service changes.

**Limits:** Uncalibrated commute-only demand proxy; non-work travel absent; no external validation data or verified physical fleet requirement. Waiting/assignment assumptions and optimizer basin dependence limit interpretation. No individual route headway is a recommendation. No COTA endorsement or external peer review is claimed.
