## Documented History

The Educational Service Center of Central Ohio's Native American History Month handout, page 3, identifies a former mound near Mound Street and High Street as the origin of Mound Street's name. This is an educational secondary account, not an excavation report. This note does not assign that destroyed mound a date or archaeological culture.

Source: [ESC of Central Ohio, Native American History Month, page 3](https://www.escco.org/Downloads/NAHM%202023%20Map-digital2.pdf?v=0). Text checked 2026-09-23.

Ohio History Connection identifies Shrum Mound as a surviving conical burial mound in Columbus, attributed to Adena people and approximately 2,000 years old. This is not a reason to label every Columbus mound Hopewell.

Source: [Ohio History Connection, Shrum Mound](https://www.ohiohistory.org/visit/browse-historical-sites/shrum-mound/). Indexed source description consulted 2026-09-23; direct page retrieval timed out. Recheck full site account before extending these claims.

For regional Hopewell ceremonial landscapes, use the separate [Hopewell source trail](hopewell.md). Adena and Hopewell are archaeological designations, not interchangeable names for every earthwork or for living Native nations.

## Word Source

The Greek roots of metropolis mean mother and city. The homepage uses that etymology, not a claim that this is the word's only modern meaning. [American Heritage Dictionary: metropolis](https://www.ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=metropolis), checked 2026-09-23.
