Transit / COTA

The stop is a room.

Question

What would make the whole city available without a car?

Fact The existing research note points to COTA's service and rider-information sources. It does not yet contain a route-by-route service baseline.

Analysis Frequency, transfers, service hours, and the walk to a stop need to be examined together. The note is still being hardened; no system-wide improvement estimate is published.

Proposal Treat shelter, seating, crossings, and legible route information as basic public-room equipment.

A separate computational route-optimization experiment is being developed and audited in public.

COTA Route Optimization

Question

How much could Columbus bus service improve through frequency allocation and network changes under explicit constraints?

Status

Independent computational experiment under active audit and hardening. The working repository is public. No final system-level finding is claimed here.

Evidence / work

Code, experiment structure, audit material, and intermediate outputs are available in the working repository. Public performance claims should wait until normalization, resource-constraint, and reproducibility checks are complete.

Limitations

A model is not an operating plan. Data quality, fleet and resource constraints, feasibility, rider impacts, and operational review must be stated before translating model results into a service recommendation. No COTA endorsement or expert review is claimed.

AI infrastructure

The machine pays tribute.

Question

What should the public receive when its land, utilities, and institutions support the machine?

Fact Existing policy / regulation: the source trail includes the AEP Ohio data-center tariff, a PUCO order, and Ohio Revised Code 122.175. These are distinct instruments, not a single civic-dividend policy.

Analysis Identified research gaps: the project has not assembled a deal-by-deal record of water demand, public costs, tax benefits, or enforceable neighborhood returns. A missing record here is not proof that no rule exists.

Proposal The AI Civic Dividend calls for disclosure, cost protection, public benefit, and access to compute. Those demands are Full City proposals, not descriptions of current law.

  • Data-center public test card

Limitations

Legal, utility, tax, and procurement review remain outstanding. No public-cost estimate or technical dataset is published here.

History / Second Geometry

Ohio was already monumental.

Question

What does this region's monumental past ask of its modern public life?

Documented history / fact The Hopewell note draws on UNESCO and the National Park Service for Ohio's ancient Indigenous ceremonial earthworks. These are regional sites, not evidence of an uninterrupted modern Columbus metropolis.

Full City interpretation / analysis Monumental public form is part of this region's history. Full City treats that scale of ambition as a challenge to the present.

Mythic language First geometry: earth. Second geometry: city. This is the project's poetic frame, not an archaeological finding or a claim of cultural succession.

  • Mound & High / Shrum Mound source trail

Limitations

No expert review is claimed. Historical, archaeological, and living Native perspectives remain necessary for further historical claims. No new historical dataset is published here.
